Abstract

We present a dual-arm robot cell that removes polishing-compound residue from a moulded polyurethane bezel, and evaluate it in simulation. Arm A holds the part in an expansion fixture, Arm B applies a compliant rotating brush under force control, and three fixed RGB-D cameras are the only source of the surface belief. Every accept decision rests on checks that do not depend on a learned model: a supervisor, a belief that keeps unobserved surface unknown, a per-cell ledger that bounds predicted wear of the plastic, and a governor and watchdog that bound a learned residual.

On a locked suite of 1,000 simulated parts the deterministic baseline accepts 92.7 % of 790 admissible parts (95 % CI 90.6–94.4) and passes none of 146 parts with visible residue, but passes 27 of 29 parts whose residue is below the cameras' colour-strength limit. Residual PPO (three seeds, 2.0 M transitions each) adds 0.6 points (CI −0.1 to +1.4) and passes the promotion rule; a ten-line scripted schedule adds 1.4 points but one new false accept, and fails it. Yield is bounded by the wear budget and by what the cameras can see, not by control.

Method

One arm holds the part, the other brushes it, and three fixed RGB-D cameras decide what is clean. The surface belief keeps every unobserved cell unknown, so QA can only pass what it has seen. A per-cell wear budget replaces pass counting: the planner doses residue until predicted wear of the clean skin reaches its cap, then sends the part to review. Learning only proposes bounded corrections, behind a governor and a watchdog.

Learning behind the governor

The planner sets the stroke and the nominal force, feed and spindle speed. The residual policy proposes rate-limited corrections to these and tangent offsets of up to 1 mm; the governor projects them onto the recipe envelope, and the watchdog zeroes any proposal that is late or invalid. An untrained or disabled policy is exactly the baseline. Each seed drew resets from 256 configurations by success-guided sampling. A learned free-space mover (ACT) was also tested against the planner it imitates.

Figure 10. Residual PPO, three seeds of 2.0 M transitions each, all reported. Validation stays between 80 and 88 %, around the untrained 86 %.

Limitations

  1. Nothing physical has been measured. Robot, fixture and tool are proxies; process coefficients are synthetic.
  2. The part is a factory sample. Reach, grasp margins and coverage must be redone for the production part.
  3. Three RGB cameras cannot certify thin or colourless residue, or the finish itself.
